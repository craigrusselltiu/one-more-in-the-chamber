# "Slick" Reno -- Second Playable Character

## Overview

**Name:** "Slick" Reno
**Species:** Raccoon
**Archetype:** Card sharp / gambler
**Sprite:** 64x64 pixel art

A raccoon card sharp in a silk vest and bowler hat. Where Russ is a scrappy gunslinger who aims true, Reno is a smooth-talking gambler who stacks the odds. He doesn't fight fair -- he fights lucky.

## Backstory

Reno ran the best poker table in the territory -- not because he was good at cards, but because nobody ever realized he wasn't playing cards at all. He was playing *them*. Rigged decks, loaded dice, a grin that made you feel like you were winning right up until you checked your pockets.

He made a lot of money. Then he made the mistake of running a game on "Copperhead" Cassidy.

Cassidy didn't lose gracefully. She doesn't lose at all. When she figured out the raccoon in the silk vest had been dealing from the bottom for three straight hours, she didn't flip the table -- she smiled, bought him a drink, and told him exactly how much he now owed her. The number was not small. The interest was worse.

Reno skipped town that night. He's been skipping towns ever since. Every time he stops long enough to breathe, one of Cassidy's collectors shows up with a polite reminder and an impolite weapon. The debt follows him like a shadow with teeth.

So he heads deeper into the Frontier, figuring the one place Cassidy's reach might not extend is past her own front door. If he can get to her and settle the debt -- hustle enough gold, win the right game, talk his way out one more time -- maybe the running stops.

But when Reno finally reaches Cassidy, she laughs. The money he stole? It was never hers. She was holding the table for someone else -- running the game on behalf of "Iron Eye" Isabella. The debt Reno's been running from belongs to the most powerful animal in the Frontier, and Cassidy was just the middleman. Settling up with Copperhead doesn't clear a thing.

Now the running stops and the real game starts. Reno has to get to the Town, get to Isabella, and somehow talk his way out of a debt to someone who holds grudges like other people hold cards -- tight and forever. Isabella doesn't forgive. Not because she can't, but because she won't. He's been improvising his whole life. This is the last hand.

*"I don't cheat. I just play a different game than everyone else. It's not my fault they don't read the rules."*

## Pixel Art Personality

- **Idle:** Flips a poker chip across his knuckles
- **Match:** Smirks and tips his hat
- **Hit:** Catches his falling bowler hat, annoyed
- **Ability:** Fans a deck of cards, then scatters them across the board
- **Boss kill:** Leans back, puts feet up on the table

## Core Tile: Chip (4th core tile, unique to Reno)

Both characters have a unique 4th core tile alongside Bullet, Iron, and Gold. Reno's is the Chip (poker chip). Six options under consideration -- pick one tile + artifact combination.

### Option A: "Ante Up" (Risk Your Health for Damage)

**Chip tile:** Each tile deals **3 damage** to the enemy but costs **1 HP** from Reno.

- Upgrade: +2 enemy damage/level (self-damage stays at 1)
- Sauce: *"You gotta pay to play."*

Creates real tension every time Chips appear on the board. Do you go for the Chip match for big damage, or avoid it to stay alive? Health becomes a spendable resource.

**Artifact: "Loaded Dice"**
*Reno only. Chip tiles no longer cost HP. Instead, gain 1 gold per Chip tile matched.*

Completely removes the downside and adds economy. The con artist rigged the game -- he's no longer paying to play, the house is paying him.

---

### Option B: "Coin Flip" (Random Outcome)

**Chip tile:** Each tile has a **50/50 chance** of generating either **2 damage** or **2 block**.

- Upgrade: +1 to both outcomes per level
- Sauce: *"Heads or tails, partner."*

Pure gambling in tile form. Over a long fight it averages out, but individual turns are unpredictable. Sometimes you desperately need block and get damage instead. Sometimes you get exactly what you needed.

**Artifact: "Double or Nothing"**
*Reno only. Chip outcomes are doubled: 4 damage or 4 block. But on damage, also take 1 damage yourself.*

Raises the stakes. The high outcome is huge (4 per tile), but the damage flip now has a cost. Gambling on top of gambling.

---

### Option C: "Daredevil" (Stronger at Low HP)

**Chip tile:** Each tile deals **1 damage**. If Reno is below 50% HP, deals **3 damage**. Below 25% HP, deals **5 damage**.

- Upgrade: +1 to each tier per level
- Sauce: *"Backed into a corner is where I do my best work."*

The gambler who's almost out of chips plays the most recklessly. Encourages a glass cannon playstyle -- stay low, hit hard. Every fight is a high-wire act.

**Artifact: "Last Call"**
*Reno only. While below 25% HP, Chip matches also heal 1 HP per tile. Chip damage thresholds shift up: below 75% = 3, below 50% = 5.*

Opens up the power tiers so you spend more of the fight in danger mode. The healing at critical HP gives just enough sustain to keep the gamble going without stabilizing you out of the bonus.

---

### Option D: "Bluff" (Read Your Opponent)

**Chip tile:** Each tile deals **1 damage**. If the targeted enemy's current intent is **Attack**, deals **3 damage** instead.

- Upgrade: +1 to both values per level
- Sauce: *"I'll see your bet and raise you."*

Poker is about reading the other player. Chips reward you for paying attention to enemy intent and timing your matches. When the enemy is about to swing big, you swing bigger. When they're blocking or buffing, Chips are just chip damage.

**Artifact: "Called Bluff"**
*Reno only. Chip bonus also triggers on enemy Block and Summon intents (not just Attack). Additionally, Chip matches against attacking enemies also grant 1 block per tile.*

Now almost every enemy intent triggers the bonus. The gambler who can read every tell is never caught off guard.

---

### Option E: "Snake Eyes" (Gamble on the Cascade)

**Chip tile:** Each tile deals **0 damage** normally. If the Chip match triggers **at least 1 cascade**, deals **4 damage** per tile instead.

- Upgrade: +2 to cascade payout per level
- Sauce: *"Let it ride."*

You're betting on the chain reaction. Sometimes you can read the board and predict the cascade. Sometimes you just pray. Either way, the moment between the match and the cascade resolving is pure gambling tension -- did it hit or bust?

**Artifact: "Loaded Dice"**
*Reno only. Chip bust (no cascade) now deals 2 damage per tile instead of 0. Cascade payout increases to 5.*

Raises the floor and the ceiling. No more total whiffs, and good reads pay off even harder.

---

### Option F: "All In" (Power Scales With Held Ability)

**Chip tile:** Each tile deals **1 damage**. While Shuffle the Deck is **fully charged** (ready to use), deals **4 damage** instead.

- Upgrade: +1 to both values per level
- Sauce: *"The best hand is the one you never play."*

The only tile in the game that gets WORSE when you use your ability. Once Shuffle is charged, Chips become devastating -- but activating the shuffle resets your charges and drops you back to 1 damage per tile. Creates a unique dilemma: do you shuffle a bad board, or keep your Chips hitting hard?

**Artifact: "Poker Face"**
*Reno only. After using Shuffle the Deck, Chip tiles deal 3 damage (instead of 1) for the next 3 turns.*

Softens the crash after shuffling. You still lose the 4-damage peak, but 3 damage for 3 turns means you're not punished as hard for actually using your ability.

---

## Ability: "Shuffle the Deck"

**Charge:** +1 per turn (passive, same as Deadeye). Threshold: **7** (lower than Deadeye's 10, since shuffle is less directly powerful).

**Activation (press Space):**
1. Enter **Hold mode** -- select up to **3 tiles** to hold (marked with a chip icon, locked in place)
2. Press Space again to confirm
3. All non-held tiles shuffle to random board positions
4. Board resolves: any matches created by the shuffle cascade normally, generating resources

**Key interactions:**
- Hold a Showdown or Explosive tile to protect it through the shuffle
- Shuffle can break a dead board when no good swaps exist
- Cascades from the shuffle generate full resources
- The shuffle itself is the value -- a board reset that creates new match opportunities

**Contrast with Deadeye:**

| | Deadeye (Russ) | Shuffle the Deck (Reno) |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | +1/turn, threshold 10 | +1/turn, threshold 7 |
| Value type | Direct (destroy tiles = immediate resources) | Setup (rearrange board = create opportunities) |
| Skill expression | Pick the 3 best tiles to shoot | Pick the 3 best tiles to hold |
| Feel | Precision marksman | Chaos with a safety net |
